About

Roots, water and crop genetics

I am a graduate researcher in the Division of Plant Science and Technology at the University of Missouri, advised by Dr. Jagdeep Singh Sidhu. My work combines root phenotyping, quantitative genetics, genome-wide association analysis and functional genomics to find and test genes that control root vascular traits in crops.

Before Missouri, I did graduate research at South Dakota State University and earned a B.Tech. in Agricultural Biotechnology at Punjab Agricultural University, where I worked on cotton and maize in the field and under the microscope.

Research

What I work on

119 Watkins wheat landraces

Finding the genes

I measure root anatomy across a diverse collection of historic wheat landraces and run genome-wide association analysis, followed by linkage disequilibrium and haplotype analyses, to narrow down candidate genes for metaxylem vessel diameter and stele size.

Wheat · rice · Arabidopsis

Testing what they do

I phenotype mutant and gene-edited lines in wheat, rice and Arabidopsis to test whether a candidate gene changes vessel size and axial hydraulic conductance, the ease with which water moves along the root.

Root Anatomy Tool (RAT)

Imaging for others

I lead the Root Anatomy Tool shared imaging resource, imaging samples and training researchers from inside and outside the University of Missouri, from parasitic dodder stems to cottonwood and maize samples.

Imaging · Root Anatomy Tool

Images from the Root Anatomy Tool

Tools · AI-assisted programming

Tools I built

Root Anatomy Tool (RAT) facility · 2026

Microscope Scale Calculator

Type the magnification shown on the microscope screen and get the scale for that image, ready to paste into ImageJ. It also converts lengths and areas from pixels to micrometres. I calibrated it by photographing a millimetre ruler at seven magnifications and built it with AI-assisted coding. Everyone using the RAT facility now uses it to set the scale for their measurements.

pixels per mm = 7.10 × magnification · R² = 0.987 · calibrated 14.7× to 175.5× Open the calculator →
